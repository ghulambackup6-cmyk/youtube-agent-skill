## Script

```
[0:00 HOOK]
VO: Before you die, you can know the 3 questions of the grave.
    Most people never learn them until it is too late.
[ON SCREEN: Slow push-in on a quiet graveyard at dusk. Fresh soil on one grave. A lantern flickers.]

VO: And the Prophet ﷺ told us exactly what they are.
[ON SCREEN: Text: "3 QUESTIONS". Wind sound.]

[0:12 THE TURN]
VO: In the next few minutes, I'll take you through what happens in the grave,
    step by step, using only authentic hadith and the Quran.
    And by the end, you'll know the 3 questions, and the answers.
[ON SCREEN: Three glowing cards, face down, numbered 1 2 3.]

[SERIES STING 3s: "Unseen Explained"]

[0:30 BEAT 1. THE FOOTSTEPS]
VO: Imagine the burial is over.
    Your family says the last du'a, turns around, and starts walking away.
    The Prophet ﷺ said that the person in the grave hears them.
[SOURCE CARD: Sahih al-Bukhari 1338]
VO: He hears their footsteps leaving.
[ON SCREEN: Low angle from the grave: silhouettes of mourners walking away. Footsteps on gravel, fading.]
VO: And it's in that moment, while their footsteps are still fading,
    that two angels come to him.
[ON SCREEN: Two tall shapes of white light appear in the darkness. No faces.]

[1:15 BEAT 2. THE TWO ANGELS]
VO: In a hadith in Jami' at-Tirmidhi, the Prophet ﷺ named them.
    Munkar. And Nakir.
[SOURCE CARD: Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1071, graded Hasan]
[ON SCREEN: Arabic calligraphy: منكر و نكير, glowing.]
VO: In Sahih al-Bukhari, he described that they sit the person up.
    No more sleep. No more waiting. The questioning begins.
[SOURCE CARD: Sahih al-Bukhari 1338]
[ON SCREEN: Inside a dim grave, a shrouded figure rising to sit, lit from above.]

[2:00 BEAT 3. THE SETTING OF THE HADITH]
VO: The fullest description comes from the companion al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, may Allah be pleased with him.
    He said they were at a funeral with the Prophet ﷺ, and he sat down at the grave.
    And before he told them anything, he asked Allah for protection from the punishment of the grave.
[SOURCE CARD: Sunan Abi Dawud 4753, graded Sahih by Al-Albani]
[ON SCREEN: Wide shot, desert cemetery, companions seated around a figure whose face is covered by soft white light.]
VO: Think about that. The Prophet ﷺ himself was asking for protection.
    So what is it that he was so careful about?

[2:45 BEAT 4. QUESTION ONE]
[ON SCREEN: First card flips: "1. Who is your Lord?"]
VO: The first question: Who is your Lord?
    The believer answers: My Lord is Allah.
VO: It sounds easy. Everyone in this video knows the answer right now.
    But this isn't a question you answer with your memory.
    It's a question you answer with your life.
[ON SCREEN: Quick montage: a man skipping prayer, scrolling at night; then a man standing for Fajr.]
VO: Think about what "my Lord" really means.
    The one you obey when nobody is watching.
    The one you turn to when you're afraid.
    The one whose pleasure you chase, or whose anger you ignore.
[ON SCREEN: A lone figure in a dark room, phone light on his face turned away, a prayer mat rolled up in the corner.]
VO: For some people, the honest answer to "who is your Lord"
    would be their money. Their phone. What people think of them.
    In the grave, there is no time to think about it.
    The answer that comes out is the one your life already wrote.
[ON SCREEN: Pages of a diary turning by themselves in the wind, words fading.]

[4:00 BEAT 5. QUESTION TWO]
[ON SCREEN: Second card flips: "2. What is your religion?"]
VO: The second: What is your religion?
    The believer says: My religion is Islam.
VO: Not the religion written on your ID card.
    Not the religion of your family name.
    Islam means submission to Allah.
    So the question is really: did you submit?
    Did your prayers, your money, your words, your choices belong to Allah?
[ON SCREEN: Hands placing a coin in a charity box at night, a lantern beside it.]

[4:45 BEAT 6. QUESTION THREE]
[ON SCREEN: Third card flips: "3. Who is this man who was sent among you?"]
VO: And the third: Who is this man who was sent among you?
    The believer says: He is the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.
[SOURCE CARD: Sunan Abi Dawud 4753]
VO: In Sahih al-Bukhari, the believer says: I testify that he is Allah's slave and His Messenger.
[SOURCE CARD: Sahih al-Bukhari 1338]
VO: Notice: the angels don't ask "did you know his name?"
    Everyone knows his name.
    The believer answers because he followed him.
    He knew his sunnah, he loved him, he sent salawat upon him.
    You can't testify for someone you never got to know.
[ON SCREEN: An old book of hadith open on a wooden stand, candlelight, pages gently lifting.]

[5:40 BEAT 7. "MY SERVANT HAS SPOKEN THE TRUTH"]
VO: And then, in the narration of al-Bara', a caller calls out from the heavens:
    "My servant has spoken the truth."
[ON SCREEN: Light pours down into the grave from above. Silence, then a single soft tone.]
VO: And in the hadith of Tirmidhi, the Prophet ﷺ said his grave is widened
    seventy cubits by seventy cubits.
[SOURCE CARD: Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1071]
[ON SCREEN: The walls of the grave move outward. Light fills the space.]

[6:25 BEAT 8. THE ONE WHO FAILS]
VO: But not everyone answers.
[ON SCREEN: Light cuts out. Darkness. Heartbeat.]
VO: The disbeliever is asked the same questions. And he says:
    "Alas, alas. I do not know."
[SOURCE CARD: Sunan Abi Dawud 4753]
VO: In Sahih al-Bukhari, the hypocrite says:
    "I do not know. I used to say what the people used to say."
[SOURCE CARD: Sahih al-Bukhari 1338]
[ON SCREEN: A crowd of grey silhouettes all mouthing the same words; one figure among them, lost.]
VO: That line should scare every one of us.
    "I used to say what the people said."
    He went along with everyone. He never made the answer his own.
    He said the words, maybe even every Friday.
    But they were never his.
VO: The Prophet ﷺ said he is struck with an iron hammer,
    and in the hadith of Tirmidhi, the earth closes in on him.
[SOURCE CARD: Sahih al-Bukhari 1338 · Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1071]
[ON SCREEN: Grave walls closing in, dust falling. No gore.]

[7:30 BEAT 9. THE BARRIER]
VO: And there is no going back.
    Allah tells us in the Quran that when death comes, a person cries out:
    "My Lord! Let me go back, so I may do good in what I left behind."
[SOURCE CARD: Quran 23:99-100, The Clear Quran]
VO: And the answer is no. Behind them is a barrier, a barzakh,
    until the Day they are raised.
[ON SCREEN: A hand reaching up through mist toward a closed gate of light.]

[8:05 BEAT 10. THE VERSE ABOUT THE GRAVE]
VO: So how does anyone pass?
    Al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, the same companion, narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari
    that this verse was revealed about the grave:
[SOURCE CARD: Sahih al-Bukhari 1369]
VO: "Allah makes the believers steadfast with the firm Word of faith
     in this worldly life and the Hereafter."
[SOURCE CARD: Quran 14:27, The Clear Quran]
[ON SCREEN: Calm night sky over the desert, one bright star, the verse reference in gold.]
VO: Steadfast. Firm.
    The answers aren't something you cram for at the last moment.
    Allah makes them firm for the people who held on to them in this life.
    The same word, in this world and in the grave.

[8:50 BEAT 11. THE DU'A HE USED TO MAKE]
VO: And the Prophet ﷺ gave us one more thing.
    Abu Hurairah narrated, in Sahih al-Bukhari,
    that the Prophet ﷺ used to make this du'a:
[SOURCE CARD: Sahih al-Bukhari 1377]
VO: "O Allah, I seek refuge with You from the punishment of the grave,
     and from the punishment of the Fire,
     and from the trial of life and death,
     and from the trial of the Masih ad-Dajjal."
[ON SCREEN: Text: "Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min 'adhabil-qabr"]
VO: If the Prophet ﷺ himself asked for this protection,
    how much more do we need it?
    Learn it tonight. Say it in your prayer.

[9:30 PAYOFF]
VO: So here they are. The 3 questions:
    Who is your Lord? What is your religion? Who is this man who was sent among you?
[ON SCREEN: All three cards side by side.]
VO: Now you know them. Before your death, not after.
    And now you know the verse, and the du'a.

[9:50 LESSON]
VO: The answers in the grave aren't memorised. They are lived.
    So the real question isn't "do you know the answers?"
    It's "will your life say them for you?"
[ON SCREEN: Back at the graveyard at dawn. The lantern still burning. Fajr adhan begins faintly.]

[10:10 CLOSE]
VO: Next, watch what the Quran says happens in Barzakh, the world between death and the Day of Judgment.
[ON SCREEN: End screen with next video.]
```

**Runtime estimate:** 930 spoken words, 6 to 7.5 minutes depending on pace. Two new beats (10 and
11) were added on 2026-11-26 from two more verified hadith (S6, S7).
