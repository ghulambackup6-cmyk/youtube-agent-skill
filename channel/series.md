# The three series

Every long video belongs to exactly one series. Each series has its own thumbnail colour, its own
opening sting and its own title pattern, so viewers learn what to expect and binge one series.

## 1. Unseen Explained (Islamic topics)

- **What it is:** one question about Islam, answered clearly from the Quran and authentic hadith.
  Leans into the unseen world (angels, jinn, the grave, the Day of Judgment, signs of the Hour)
  because those topics overlap with the horror audience.
- **Length:** 8 to 12 minutes.
- **Title pattern:** a question or a surprising claim. "What Happens in the First Night of the
  Grave?", "Why Did Iblis Refuse to Bow?"
- **Look:** dark blue and gold. Calm. Text cards for every ayah and hadith on screen, with the
  reference.
- **Rule:** no scholarly opinion is stated as fact unless it is sourced. Where scholars differ,
  say so.
- **First topics:** the questioning in the grave; who are Munkar and Nakir; what jinn can and
  cannot do; the 10 major signs of the Hour; what Barzakh is.

## 2. Night Tales (Islamic horror)

- **What it is:** chilling stories built on Islamic ideas of the unseen: jinn, the grave, evil
  eye, haunted places, last moments. Two kinds, always labelled in the first 30 seconds and in
  the description:
  - **Story:** a fictional story inspired by Islamic teachings. Said clearly as fiction.
  - **Narration:** something actually reported in hadith or classical books, with the source and
    its grading.
- **Length:** 10 to 15 minutes. Horror holds attention longer.
- **Title pattern:** a situation plus a twist. "He Heard Footsteps After His Own Burial", "The
  Jinn Who Prayed Behind Him".
- **Look:** near-black with red and white. Fog, lanterns, empty streets, graveyards at night.
- **Rule:** every story ends with the lesson and the real ayah or hadith behind it. No black
  magic instructions, no rituals, nothing that mocks the religion. No real people accused.
- **First topics:** the night in the grave; the man who did not believe in the jinn; the house
  where Surah Al-Baqarah was never recited; the last words of a dying man.

## 3. Forgotten Pages (Islamic history)

- **What it is:** dramatic, true stories from Islamic history: battles, scholars, rulers,
  inventors, cities. Told like a documentary.
- **Length:** 10 to 15 minutes.
- **Title pattern:** a name, a date or a number. "The Day Baghdad Fell (1258)", "The 9-Year-Old
  Who Memorised Sahih al-Bukhari".
- **Look:** sand, parchment and deep green. Maps, old manuscripts, cities.
- **Rule:** historical claims need a named source (a classical book like Ibn Kathir's *al-Bidaya
  wan-Nihaya* or a modern academic work). Weak or disputed reports are labelled as such.
- **First topics:** the fall of Baghdad 1258; Salahuddin and Jerusalem 1187; the House of Wisdom;
  Ibn Battuta's journey; the burning of the Library of Cordoba (myth or fact?).

## Prophets and Sahaba on screen

Allowed only as animated characters whose face is fully covered by a soft white light or glow,
as you decided. The pipeline will also:
- never give them a voice line spoken "as" them. The narrator quotes them ("he said, peace be
  upon him...").
- add ﷺ / (AS) / (RA) on screen after names.
- keep the face covered in every frame, including thumbnails. You check this at approval.

## Shorts

Seven a week. Most are cut from the long videos with `/yt-shorts`. Every Short gets a new first
line and points to the long video. The same files go to Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels and
TikTok.
